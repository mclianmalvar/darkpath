Questions from the gate

What Dark Path is, when you can play it, and how its hardest rules work.

The game Release Playing

The game

What is Dark Path?

A 2D dark-fantasy tower defense, city builder and dungeon crawler. You rule Greywatch, the last walled city of a fallen kingdom. Before each invasion you get a countdown of days to build defenses and send heroes into dungeons for power and loot. Then you hold the walls in a real-time siege.

Who is making it?

Mc Lian M. Malvar, as a solo project covering design, programming, art and sound. Friends and classmates playtest each milestone.

What is it made with?

Godot 4.3, with code in GDScript. The first playable version was built for the web browser and is being moved to Godot. The Godot source uses 128 by 128 pixel textures. Its core defenders have detailed new character artwork with layered armor, sculpted faces, front/back/side views and distinct weapons. Recruits use equipment-matched templates with their own colors. Enemies and buildings have finer material detail. Their drawing size in the world stays the same, and walk cycles, strikes and collapses remain intact. Terrain tiles, portraits and interface icons keep their own resolutions. The downloadable playtest build still uses the earlier art. The music and ambience are composed in code and play from the game itself.

How long is a campaign?

Four invasions across four acts, about 15 to 20 hours. A countdown day takes about 3 to 10 minutes, longer with a dungeon run, and an invasion about 20 to 30 minutes. In the current build all four acts can be played, at full length (countdowns of 30, 30, 25 and 20 days) or as a short campaign (10, 10, 9 and 8), chosen at a new game.

Release

When does it come out?

There is no release date. Dark Path is built at a hobby pace with no fixed deadlines: each milestone starts when the last one's goal is met. The design is finished, and the whole campaign, Prologue to the Unmade King, is built in Godot as a playtest. The roadmap shows every step.

Can I play it now?

Yes: a free playtest build for Windows is on the Download page. It covers the Prologue and all four acts, with their invasions, all four dungeon regions, every hero, every defense, siege engine and system in the design, Endless mode and two Challenges. Expect rough edges, and report what you find on GitHub, ideally with the battle logs the game saves after every battle (the battle summary shows where they are). The devlog announces new builds.

Which platforms?

PC (Windows), played with keyboard and mouse. All text is in English.

How much will it cost?

That isn't decided yet. The options on the table are a free release on itch.io, a free demo with a paid full game, or a paid release on Steam.

Are there other modes?

Yes, and they are in the playtest build. Endless plays the campaign, and after Invasion IV the Hollow keep coming: every countdown is shorter than the last and every host larger, and you are ranked by how many days you survive. Two Challenges have fixed rules: in No Walls the walls and gates fall when Act 1 begins and can never be rebuilt, and in No Dungeons the Root is sealed, so there are no expeditions and the heroes found below never come. Pick the mode at a new game; the title screen keeps your best Endless run and every Challenge you have won.

Playing

Is hero death really permanent?

Yes, on every difficulty. In an invasion, a downed hero can be saved if any ally reaches them within 15 seconds. In a dungeon, a hero at 0 HP is downed, and a second hit kills them. The only exception is the Prologue, where nobody dies.

The dark can take a hero another way: Hollow Taint builds up below the Root, and a hero whose Taint reaches 100 turns Hollow and is lost. Mother Ilse or a Sanctum can cleanse it before then.

The names of the fallen are carved into the Memorial Wall, and each leaves something behind for the rest of the campaign: a fallen recruit raises the floor under the city's morale, and a fallen story hero leaves a gift of their own, such as Sera's farther-reaching Archer Posts. Their bonded friends grieve them.

Can I save and reload?

No. Every difficulty is autosave only, with one save per campaign. The game saves at each Dawn, after each dungeon battle and whenever a hero dies. Quitting saves and exits. This is what makes every death count.

How hard is it?

There are three difficulties: Steward (smaller invasions and more starting resources), Warden (the intended experience) and Tyrant (stronger invasions and tighter resources). On every difficulty, losses carry forward and there is no comeback help after a defeat.

What are Decrees?

Harsh laws you pass from the city's Decrees window, one at a time. Conscription lets militia squads grow from 8 soldiers to 12 for 15 morale. A Curfew keeps any Mimic from slipping in among refugees, at 10% of the city's production. Plunder the Depths brings home half again as much gold and stone from the Old Mines, but every hero goes down with more Dread. Open the Gates lets refugees in every day to work, and sometimes a Mimic with them. From Act 2, Burn the Outskirts burns the east track so the Hollow can only come down the north road, at the cost of every building in the Farm Ring.

Passing a Decree repeals the one in force, and every Decree you pass is counted as Tyranny (Burn the Outskirts counts twice). Break the last invasion with 5 Tyranny or more and Greywatch survives as a Tyrant's city; with less, as a Guardian's.

Do I need fast reflexes?

Not necessarily. Dungeon battles use timed strikes and parries, but the Auto-timing setting resolves them for you at about 60% of the timing bonus, on any difficulty. Invasions run in real time with an active pause, so you can stop the battle to give orders.

How do I command units in a siege?

Click or drag to select heroes, militia squads and the Warden, then right-click to move or attack. To set a formation, hold the right mouse button and drag a line, as in Total War: a long line spreads your troops wide and a short one stacks them in ranks. Hold Shift while you give an order to queue it after the ones already given.

Units reach the top of the walls only by the wall stairs. Up there they shoot farther and the ground horde cannot reach them, but Gloomwings dive at them, a Brute or Gravemaw hitting the wall below shakes them, and when that stretch of wall breaks they fall to the ground, hurt, among the monsters. Space pauses the battle, and orders still work while it is paused. In every invasion, V uses Vael's Whisper on one of the Hollow, G sets off Habyr's Hatred, Q casts Isolde's Frostfire on an area, E has Mother Ilse hold her Vigil, and L lets a downed hero make a last stand. T switches a siege engine's ammo. Every key can be changed under Settings, Controls.

Spearmen have a skill of their own: select a squad, press P and click where it should face, and it forms a Phalanx, shields locked in ranks. It holds there, its spears reach farther, it hits harder and takes half the damage of every blow from the front, but it strikes only the Hollow in front of it and will not chase. A move order, or P again, breaks it. Siege engines fire on their own, but only into their firing arc, the wedge in front of them that shows in orange when you select one: left-click to turn an engine (it swings round at its own pace), or click one of the Hollow to point it there. A Catapult's or Trebuchet's shot bursts in a cone that sprays on past where it lands, away from the engine.

You can give the same orders during the countdown: your troops stand in the city, so you can post them on the walls or behind a gate before the Hollow arrive. Where they stand when the last day ends is where the invasion begins.

Move the camera with W A S D or the arrow keys, by dragging with the middle mouse button, or by touching the edge of the screen with the mouse. Edge scrolling can be turned off in the settings.

What do the weather and the Omens do?

Every invasion comes in its own weather, and from the second one under an Omen; the scout report tells you both before the horn. Rain halves fire, fog shortens every range, snow slows everyone and the siege engines' crews, and wind sends arrows astray. A Blood Moon brings a larger host but pays a Gold for every kill, Ashfall shortens the towers' reach, a Starless night blinds the Watchtowers, and the Hunger makes the Hollow faster.

The Hollow also learn. If one kind of tower deals more than 40% of the damage in an invasion you win, the next host takes 30% less damage from it, and the battle summary warns you. Spread the work across your defenses.

How do expeditions work?

Click the Root, the great tree in the western woods, to send up to four heroes down through the doorway in it. Pick a region (one more opens each act: the Old Mines, the Drowned Crypts, the Ashen Warrens and the Root of the Maw) and a length. A Short run takes a day. A Medium run takes two days and ends at the region's boss; a Long run takes three, ends at the boss, and makes camp halfway. The extra days pass as soon as the party is home, while the city works on without them. Each boss drops a Relic once per campaign.

Heroes join along the way: Habyr in the Old Mines, Isolde in the Drowned Crypts, and Mother Ilse when the Farm Ring is retaken. Build an Inn to hire recruits of six classes, each with a quirk or two, up to eight heroes in all.

Heroes who come home together, or win an invasion together, grow close: Acquainted, then Trusted, then Sworn. Side by side they hit harder, and a Sworn pair has a pair skill in the dungeon, on key 5. Every kind of Hollow you kill fills in its page in the Bestiary, under Orders, until at 50 kills you have studied it and hit it harder.

Who is it for?

Players 13 and up who enjoy tower defense, strategy, dungeon crawlers and dark fantasy. The rating target is Teen: fantasy violence, no gore.
