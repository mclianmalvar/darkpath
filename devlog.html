Oct 25, 2026
ArtSource update

A richer view of Greywatch

The Godot source now has a new title vista: a detailed fortress above fractured cliffs, layered mountains, distant ruins, warm windows and red standards. The Maw glows above the sea, with broken reflections and drifting ash.

  • Quieter ground. Softer patches of color replace much of the fine terrain noise, helping troops stand out.
  • More building detail. Individual roof shingles, recessed windows with warm light, and damp stonework give the city more depth.
  • A consistent palette. Muted teal water and cooler paving balance the amber windows and firelight.

This is an art update to the source project. The downloadable Windows build has not been replaced by this update. Combat rules, controls and balance are unchanged.

Before-and-after views were checked in Godot 4.3. The automated run reached the end screen with 419 passing checks and no script errors; its campaign ended in Act III, so later campaign play remains unverified.

Oct 21, 2026
ReleaseBattle

Firing arcs for the siege engines

The siege engines now fight the way line infantry do in Total War. In the playtest build on the Download page:

  • Every engine has a firing arc. Select a Catapult, Trebuchet or Cannon and its arc shows in orange: a wedge in front of it, from the closest it can hit to the farthest. 80 degrees wide for the Catapult, 60 for the Trebuchet, 70 for the Cannon.
  • It fires on its own at the Hollow inside its arc, at the thickest knot of them, and holds its fire, and your ammo, while the arc is empty.
  • Turning it takes time. Left-click to turn an engine toward that point; it swings round at its own pace while its crew is at it, the Trebuchet slowest. Click one of the Hollow to point it at that one. Engines start facing north, toward the Hollow, or out from the city when they stand on a wall.

In our scripted test battles an engine that picks its own targets inside its arc wastes fewer shots: the bot's city held the first invasion 7 to 10 times in 10 depending on how it fought, and Invasions II and III nearly every time. Invasion IV, the Unmade King's, is as hard as it was: the bot holds it about 4 times in 10.

Oct 21, 2026
ReleaseBattle

Spears and shot: the Phalanx, and siege blasts in a cone

Two changes to how the invasions are fought, in the playtest build on the Download page:

  • Siege blasts are cones. A Catapult's or Trebuchet's shot no longer bursts in a circle. It lands where you aim and sprays on past that spot, away from the engine, in a cone: about 3 tiles long for the Catapult and more than 4 for the Trebuchet, hardest where it lands. A Fire Pot sets the whole cone burning. Aim just short of the Hollow, and a shot down the road catches the ones behind the first. A selected engine shows its cone, and the Cannon shows Iron Shot's line and Grapeshot's spray.
  • The Phalanx. Select a Spearmen squad, press P and click where it should face. The squad closes up in ranks and locks shields: its spears reach over the front rank, it hits a quarter harder, each soldier holds one more Hollow, and every blow from the front does half its damage. But it strikes only what is in front of it and will not chase, so a Hollow that gets behind the line is free to hit it. A move order, or P again, breaks it.

In our scripted test battles the Phalanx did not win more invasions, but it nearly stopped the dying: a line locked in front of the gate lost about one soldier a battle where it used to lose seven to thirteen. The Hollow that slip past it are the heroes' and the towers' to deal with. The cone blasts left the invasions about as hard as they were, a little easier where the engine fires down the road the Hollow come by.

ReleaseModes

Endless mode and the Challenges

Endless was planned as an update after launch. It is in the playtest build on the Download page now, with two Challenges beside it. Pick the mode at a new game, next to the length and the difficulty:

  • Endless. The campaign as before, but after Invasion IV the Maw stays open. Act V, VI and on: every countdown shorter than the last (8 days, then 7, 6, down to 4 in a Short campaign, twice that in a Full one), and every host larger, the hosts of the Mother of Swarms, the Pale Twins and the Unmade King coming round again in turn. You are ranked by how many days you survive.
  • No Walls. When Act 1 begins, every wall and gate falls to rubble, and none can be rebuilt. No Barricades either: towers and troops alone.
  • No Dungeons. The Root is sealed. No expeditions, no loot, no Relics, and the heroes found below never come.
  • Records. The title screen keeps your best Endless run (the days, and the act it reached) and every Challenge you have won, with the hardest difficulty you won it on.

In our scripted test battles, the bot's city holds the first two Endless acts (the Mother's and the Twins' hosts, larger than before) 10 and 8 times in 10, and then the Unmade King's host comes back in Act VII, 60% larger, and ends its run 9 times in 10. How far can you get?

ReleaseSystems

Weather and Omens, Shelters, the Bestiary, bonds, Taint and the Memorial

The last systems in the design are in the playtest build on the Download page:

  • Weather. Every invasion comes in clear skies, rain, fog, snow or wind, and the scout report tells you which. Rain halves fire, fog shortens every range, snow slows everyone and the siege engines' crews, and wind sends arrows astray and speeds the Gloomwings.
  • Omens, from the second invasion: a Blood Moon (a larger host, but every kill pays a Gold), Ashfall (shorter tower range), Starless (the Watchtowers go blind) or the Hunger (faster Hollow).
  • The Hollow adapt. If one kind of tower deals more than 40% of the damage in an invasion, the next host takes 30% less damage from it. The battle summary and the next scout report warn you.
  • Shelters in the Inner Keep hold 25 evacuated citizens each. Crowd more in, and morale drops and a fever may follow.
  • The Bestiary, under Orders: a page for every kind of Hollow, filled in as you kill them. Its numbers at 10 kills, its weakness at 25, and at 50 you have studied it and hit it 5% harder.
  • Bonds and Grief. Heroes who come home from the dark or win an invasion together grow close: Acquainted, Trusted, then Sworn. Side by side they hit harder, a Trusted partner eases the Dread, and a Sworn pair gets a pair skill in the dungeon (key 5). When a hero dies, their bonded partners grieve; Habyr turns his grief into fury.
  • Hollow Taint. The dark and the elites' blows corrupt a hero. A little makes them stronger, more makes them strike at friends, and at 100 they are lost and rise against the party. The camp's Prayer, Mother Ilse or a Sanctum can cleanse it.
  • The Memorial Wall, under Orders, keeps every fallen hero's record, and each leaves something behind: a fallen recruit raises the floor under the city's morale, and a fallen story hero leaves a gift of their own.

In our scripted test battles, the weather, the Omens and above all Hollow that learned to resist our test cities' Ballistas made Invasions II to IV harder again: the bot now holds them about 8, 6 to 7, and 5 to 7 times in 10, close to where they were first tuned. The bot never changes its towers when the Hollow adapt, and never builds a Shelter or grows a bond. You can.

ReleaseDefenses

The full arsenal: five new defenses, branches, synergies, the Trebuchet and the Cannon

Every defense on the website's list is now in the playtest build on the Download page:

  • Five more defenses, under More in the Defenses bar (keys 8, 9 and 0 for the first three). The Flame Pit sets the ground Hollow around it burning. The Arcane Spire fires magic bolts that armor does not stop. The Spike Trap lies on the road, where the Hollow walk over it, and springs once a battle. The Watchtower sees Burrowers under the walls, marks the Hollow for your Ballistas, and catches a Mimic among the refugees before it strikes. The Chapel heals the defenders around it and lifts the city's morale every dawn.
  • A choice at the top. Every defense's last upgrade is one of two branches, for good: a Longbow Nest or a Crossbow Hall, a Piercer or a Harpoon that drags its target back, a Glacier or a Chill Aura, an Oil Trench or an Inferno, a Chain Bolt or an Aether Lance, Rearming or Poison Spikes, a Signal Fire or a Sniper Roost, a Sanctum or a Bell of Courage, a Thorn Wall or an Iron Wall.
  • Synergies. A Hollow frozen solid shatters under a heavy shot. Fire sets an Oil Trench ablaze, and the next one with it. A Ballista bolt on a Hollow a Watchtower marks is a critical hit.
  • The Trebuchet and the Cannon, built at a Workshop and crewed like the Catapult. The Trebuchet throws from a wall platform across half the map but cannot hit anything close. The Cannon comes in Act 3: Iron Shot through a whole line of Hollow, Grapeshot in a spray at the gate, and a roar that brings the Gloomwings down on its crew.
  • Four kinds of ammo: Stone Shot, Fire Pots that leave the ground burning, Iron Shot and Grapeshot. T switches what a selected engine fires.

In our scripted test battles, the city that spends its budget on towers held Invasions II and III every time and Invasion IV nine times in ten. A city that spends it first on a Workshop, the Cannon, Flame Pits, Spike Traps, a Watchtower and a Chapel held all of them every time, but lost more militia, probably because fewer towers thin the hordes before they reach it. The new defenses are a trade, not a free upgrade. The invasions are not retuned yet, because the weather and Omens coming next make every battle harder.

ReleaseDungeonsHeroes

Into the deep: three new regions, Isolde, Mother Ilse and the Inn

The dungeons now go as deep as the story does. The playtest build on the Download page has every region and every hero:

  • Three more regions, one opening each act. The Drowned Crypts hold the Risen, drowned knights, and chests that are sometimes Mimics. The Ashen Warrens hold Ash Hounds and the Maw's cultists, who heal their own, and somewhere near the end of the first run there, the truth about who opened the Maw. The Root of the Maw holds Rootspawn and Maw Knights. Each region is deadlier than the last, and pays better.
  • Short, Medium and Long runs. A Short run takes a day, as before. A Medium run takes two days and ends at the region's boss; a Long run takes three, ends at the boss, and makes camp halfway, where the party spends its camp points on bandages, a watch, sharpened blades, stories or a prayer. The extra days pass as soon as they are home.
  • Bosses and Relics. The Tide Abbess, the Ash Matron and the Heart of the Maw each fight with skills of their own, calling up more of their kind and striking the whole party at once. Each drops a Relic, a trinket like no other. Destroy the Heart, and the Unmade King comes to the last invasion weaker, with half of his dead.
  • Isolde and Mother Ilse. Isolde, a hedge witch, waits in the Drowned Crypts; in the city she sells charms, and in battle her Frostfire (Q) freezes the Hollow, then burns them. Mother Ilse comes home with the farmers when you retake the Farm Ring; resting heroes lose more Dread while she is in the city, and her Vigil (E) heals everyone around her and gets a downed hero back up.
  • The Inn. Build one to hire recruits: warriors, clerics, rangers, bards, doctors and wizards, each with a generated name and face, a quirk or two, and a class's own skills in the dungeon, in the city and on the walls. Up to eight heroes on the roster, four to a party.

How hard is it? I tested every region and run length with a scripted party at the level a Short campaign's heroes would have by then: it came home every time, boss and all, with more Dread and less health the deeper and longer it went. Two levels lower, it still did. A party that goes down hurt or afraid, or that fights without thinking, can still be lost, so watch the Dread.

Next up: the other defenses (the Flame Pit, the Arcane Spire, Spike Traps, the Watchtower and the Chapel), a choice of two branches at every defense's last step, and the Trebuchet and the Cannon.

ReleaseStoryGodot

The whole campaign: Acts 2 to 4 and the Unmade King

Breaking Invasion I no longer ends the game. The playtest build on the Download page now runs the whole campaign: the Prologue and four acts, each a countdown and an invasion, to one of two endings. What's new:

  • Three more acts. After every invasion the dead are counted and the next countdown begins. Ruins, wounds and losses carry over; nothing is handed back. A new game is a Full campaign (countdowns of 30, 30, 25 and 20 days) or a Short one (10, 10, 9 and 8).
  • Three more bosses. The Mother of Swarms births Husks every few seconds for as long as she lives, and each brood splits her brood-sac open for a moment. The Pale Twins come down both roads at once: kill one and the other enrages, unless it falls within eight seconds. The Unmade King, the old king reborn in the Maw, changes twice as he weakens: his crown breaks and his dead guard rises, and then nothing is left of him but fury.
  • Six new Hollow. Spitters spit bile at defenders on the walls. Burrowers tunnel under them, where nothing can touch them. Shriekers shield and hurry the Hollow around them. From the second invasion come the siege beasts: Climbers that go over the walls, Rams that go for the gates, and Bile Mortars that bombard your towers from beyond their reach.
  • The Farm Ring. From Act 2 you can retake the fields north of the walls: the farmers come home and Farms there yield half again as much, but they stand in the Hollow's way.
  • Two more Decrees and both endings. Open the Gates lets refugees in every day, Mimics and all. Burn the Outskirts closes the east track by burning the Farm Ring. Every Decree you pass is Tyranny, and Tyranny decides whether you save the city as a Tyrant or as its Guardian.
  • The story to its end. Cassian tells the Market what the Warden did at the capital gate, and you choose how to answer him. In Act 3 the Maw opens over the rest of the world, every invasion after that is a quarter larger, and the heroes come back from below with the truth about who opened it.
  • Twenty city events, nine of them new, and each dealt only when it fits the city.

How hard is it? I tuned each new invasion against the same scripted defender I use for Invasion I, fighting from the city a Short campaign would have by then. It holds Invasion II nine times in ten, Invasion III about six in ten, and the Unmade King about two in three. It never passes a Decree and fights the bosses without much thought, so you should do better. These are the battles I most need played by people: if one breaks you, or feels too easy, please send me its battle log.

Next up: the other three dungeon regions, the Drowned Crypts, the Ashen Warrens and the Root of the Maw, with Isolde and Mother Ilse.

PlaytestGodot

Help me balance it: battle logs and first-time hints

The playtest build on the Download page has been updated with two things that make playtesting work better:

  • Battle logs. After every battle the game saves a short text report: how long each wave took, who killed what, what it cost, and the city and army that fought it. The battle summary tells you where it is and can open the folder. If you play, please send the logs along with what you thought. Every balance number so far comes from a scripted player, and yours are worth more.
  • First-time hints. The newer mechanics now explain themselves once, the first time they matter: Gravemaw's open cracks, a hero going down, Vael's Whisper, Habyr's Hatred, a Mimic inside the walls, and in the city the Bait Cart and the Decrees. Settings turns them off, or shows them all again.

The balance bot learned the newer tools too: it now builds a Bait Cart in front of the North gate, turns a Brute with the Whisper, and saves Overclock, the Rain and Hatred for Gravemaw's open cracks. It holds Invasion I about as often as the plain defense (8 and 9 battles of 12) and loses fewer militia doing it, so the tools help without making the siege a sure thing.

ReleaseArtGodot

Act 1 in full, and a build you can play

The biggest update yet, and the first one you can download. The Act 1 playtest build for Windows is on the new Download page. What's new in it:

  • Hand-drawn heroes and monsters. The Warden, Bram, Sera, the Spearmen and all six Hollow are redrawn by hand at 32 by 32, front, back and side. They walk with a two-step stride, strike with a pose of their own, and collapse when they fall: the Hollow slump into ash, defenders topple.
  • Music and ambience. A quiet theme for the city, war drums and horns for the sieges, a low drone for the Old Mines, and wind and fire underneath. All of it is composed in code. Settings turn the music and the ambience on or off and set the volume.
  • Habyr joins. On the first expedition the party finds him fighting alone in the Old Mines: eighteen, from the burned village of Sabali, a hatchet in each hand. He fights in the dungeon, where every blow he lands builds his Hatred, and on the walls, where Hatred (G) makes each kill hit harder.
  • The first Decrees. Conscription grows your squads from 8 to 12 soldiers for 15 morale. A Curfew keeps Mimics out but costs a tenth of the city's work. Plunder the Depths brings home more gold and stone, and more Dread. One at a time, and every one you pass is remembered as Tyranny.
  • Act 1 at its full length. At a new game you now choose the full 30-day countdown, as designed, or the short 10-day version. The full one has nine city events, four of them new, the scout report on Day 25, and a bigger Invasion I to match a city built for three times as long: more Hollow, a fourth wave and a tougher Gravemaw.
  • Settings and help. Rebind every key, change the interface size, and switch to colorblind-friendly health bars. Tooltips on the buttons and hero cards, and a summary after every battle. The Root now stands on the map: click the great tree to set out.

Habyr makes the walls easier to hold. In the balance tests a scripted defender now holds Invasion I about two times in three, up from a little under half, even with Gravemaw given 300 more health. The full-length Act 1's bigger horde was tuned against a city built over thirty days to land in the same place. A player has more to work with than the script does: it never passes a Decree, builds a Bait Cart or uses the Whisper.

The press kit now has screenshots and a short clip of a siege.

ProgressGodot

Nine new ways to fight for Greywatch

A big update to the sieges and the days before them:

  • Battle honors. After every battle the report names its MVP, by kills, damage and rescues, and lists everyone's kills. Heroes who fought gain experience, the MVP more.
  • Veteran squads. Squads that live through a battle become Blooded, then Veteran, with more health and damage. Lose a Veteran squad and you feel it.
  • Gravemaw's weak spot. Each time it smashes a wall or gate, the cracks in its hide open for a moment, and every blow lands deep. Vael's scouts tell you about it. The rest of the time its hide is tougher than before.
  • Bait Carts. A new cheap defense: a cart of blood and offal that nearby Hollow go for before anything else. Put one in front of a gate, under your towers.
  • Vael's Whisper. Once per invasion, Vael turns one of the Hollow against its own kind for twenty seconds, then it crumbles. Each Whisper deepens Vael's Taint.
  • The Watch Horn. The last gallery of the Old Mines holds pieces of an old watch horn. Each piece strengthens the Warden, and the whole horn turns Overclock into a Rallying Horn for the towers and defenders around him.
  • Tomas's foresight. When the countdown begins, Tomas tells you when the Hollow will come and by which road. He is usually right. Vael's scout report tells you where he was not.
  • Refugees and Mimics. Refugees at the gate now bring fighters who form a new squad. Sometimes a Mimic hides among them. Vael can root it out, at the cost of the Whisper, or you can face it inside the walls.
  • A last stand. A downed hero can spend their last seconds on their feet: nothing brings them down for twelve seconds, then they are gone. The Memorial Wall keeps the deed, and every defender fights harder for the rest of the battle.

Outside its weak moment Gravemaw now has more health and a tougher hide. In the balance tests Invasion I holds about as often as before, so the weak spot rewards timing rather than simply making the boss easier. The roadmap now shows this update, and the next one: hand-drawn heroes and monsters at 32 by 32.

StoryGodot

The story, as it really begins

The world of Dark Path has a written lore now, and the game tells its story the way the lore does:

  • A new opening. A new campaign starts with how Veyra fell: its rotten peace, a sign in the sky that grew every night, the Hollow pouring out, and the leader of Greywatch, who was no longer the man he had been. A man from another world had woken in his place. He named the Maw, prepared the city, and chose you to be its Warden.
  • Tomas Reed is your advisor. He is that leader. He brings the dawn report every morning and helps you through each day, and he never gives the orders himself.
  • The Root. Heroes reach the Old Mines through a doorway in a great tree near the city, and nothing down there can follow them back out.
  • Only Hollow below. The dungeon's human deserters are gone. In their place is the Spitter, a crouched Hollow that spits bile from the back ranks, and the Hollow Miner is now a Pit Husk, since the Hollow were never people.
  • No lords left. Cassian Mare is no longer a lord, because no nobles survived the fall of Veyra. He is still your rival.

Battles play exactly as before. Only the words and two dungeon monsters changed.

ProgressArtGodot

Post your troops before the horn, and a sharper Greywatch

Two changes this time: one to how you prepare, one to how the city looks.

  • Your army waits in the city. During the countdown the Warden, Bram, Sera and the Spearmen stand in Greywatch, and you command them just as in a siege: click or drag to select, right-click to move, drag a formation, hold Shift to queue, H to hold position, and right-click the Catapult with a squad to crew it. They still climb the walls only by the stairs.
  • Where they stand is where the battle starts. When a day ends, every unit's post is remembered, and the invasion begins with everyone already in place. You can't build on a spot where your troops are standing.
  • The map at 32 by 32. Every tile now has four times as many pixels. The walls, gates, gatehouse arches and wall stairs, the Keep, all six buildings and every tier of every tower are redrawn by hand: stone courses, flagstone walkways, iron-banded gates under round arches, and cracks once a wall is badly hurt. Grass, trees, boulders, cottages, crops and the sea cliff are repainted too, and the grass no longer stops at a hard line where it meets a road.
  • The people come next. Heroes, militia and the Hollow are sharpened automatically for now. They will be redrawn by hand at the new size.

None of this changes how battles play: ranges, speeds and the balance numbers stay as they were. Units with no orders still start a battle at their usual posts.

ProgressGodot

Every job costs days, and a Workshop

The countdown is the real currency of Dark Path, and now everything you do to the city spends it:

  • Work costs days, and they pass at once. Upgrading a tower, repairing anything, clearing a ruin or rebuilding a wall takes days, and those days go by as soon as the work starts. Workers still bring in their output each night, the heroes rest, and the events and the scout report still come, but the invasion is that much closer.
  • How long. A repair takes a day, or two if the structure is below half health. Upgrades take one or two days, clearing a ruin one and rebuilding a wall two. Work that would not finish before the Hollow arrive cannot start.
  • The Workshop. A new building that forges better weapons and armor, two steps of each, for the Spearmen (every squad at once), the Warden, Bram and Sera. Weapons hit harder in sieges and in the dungeon. Armor takes a share off every hit in battle and adds protection in the dungeon. Forging costs days too.

In the balance tests, a city whose defenders all had the first step of weapons and armor held Invasion I a little more often and lost about half as many militia on the ground. Forging all of that takes eight of the ten days, though, so you will have to choose.

Everyone faces north

The map turned to face north a while ago, but the people and machines on it still looked sideways. Now they face the Hollow:

  • Four ways to look. The Warden, Bram, Sera and the Spearmen now have a back view and a front view as well as their side view.
  • They face where they walk. Walking north they show their backs, walking south you see their faces, and east or west they turn side-on. In a fight they face the monster they are hitting, and once they reach their post they turn north to watch for the Hollow.

BalanceGodot

The walls are no longer safe

Putting every soldier on top of the walls had become the answer to every siege: up there the ground horde could not touch them. Now it is a choice with a price:

  • Gloomwings hunt the walls. The flyers go for defenders standing on the walls before anyone else nearby. Catapult crews are still their first target.
  • Blows shake the walkway. When a Brute or Gravemaw smashes a wall or gate, the defenders standing on or right beside it are hurt too.
  • Broken walls drop their defenders. When a stretch of wall breaks, everyone on it falls to the ground. The fall hurts but never kills, and down there the monsters can reach them again.
  • A bigger surprise siege. The Prologue sends one more Brute and a few more Husks and Runners.
  • Stuck monsters, take two. A monster that stops getting closer now drops everything and takes the single best way in. It also catches a rarer case the last fix missed.

To see what this does, the game now has a balance test that plays each battle many times with scripted defenders. If you give no orders at all, the Prologue now holds in 15 of 36 tries (it was 24 of 36). A steady defense on the ground or on the walls still holds nearly every time. Holding Invasion I from the walls used to cost no militia at all; now it costs about six. Invasion I still needs a city built up during the countdown.

Real fonts, an RTS camera and battles that always end

A round of polish and fixes for the Godot build, found by playing it through screen by screen:

  • The real fonts. The game now uses Pixelify Sans and Jacquard 12, the same pixel fonts as this site, instead of plain system fonts.
  • An RTS camera. Drag with the middle mouse button to move the map, or touch the edge of the screen with the mouse. Edge scrolling can be turned off in the settings.

  • A downed hero is hard to miss. Their card pulses red until an ally reaches them.
  • Walkways over the gates. Units on the walls can now walk over every gate, so a tower built right beside a gate no longer cuts the North wall in two.
  • Fixed: battles that never ended. Once in a while a monster got stuck where no tower or soldier could reach it, so the last wave never finished. Monsters that stop getting closer now find their way again, and they can no longer wander into pockets between the trees.

The automated test now plays the way a player would: it builds Ballistas and Archer Posts along the North wall during the countdown and holds the North gate with both squads. In its last 12 runs it held the Prologue every time and beat Gravemaw in 11. The one loss came after a costly Prologue left the squads short and the timber ran out early, so Invasion I can be won, but not on autopilot.

ProgressGodot

Formations, queued orders and a third gate

The first round of changes to how sieges play in the Godot build:

  • Formations. Hold the right mouse button and drag a line, the way Total War does it. Before you let go, a preview shows the line, which way it faces and where every soldier will stand. Drag left to right and your troops face north, toward the Hollow. A long line spreads them wide, and a short one stacks them in ranks. It works on the ground and along the top of a wall.
  • Queued orders. Hold Shift while you give an order, and it waits until the units finish what they are already doing, like in other RTS games. Queued routes show as dashed lines with a marker at each stop.
  • A thicker North wall. The North wall is now two tiles thick. Units on it can walk its whole length and pass each other, and a tower built on the wall no longer blocks the walkway.
  • A North-west gate. A third gate in the west part of the North wall, reached by a branch of the main road. Units on the ground walk through it, and units on the walkway cross over it.
  • A horde, not a queue. The Hollow no longer march in single file. Each one keeps to its own side of the road, so they come as a loose crowd across the road and the fields beside it, and bunch up only at gates and gaps in the trees.

All of it passes the automated test the build runs, and it was recorded working in the game. It still has to be played by hand.

Act 1 is built, and it's moving to Godot

The first playable version of Dark Path, the MVP, is built in the browser. It covers the start of the campaign: the surprise siege on Day 1, a shorter 10-day countdown, expeditions into the Old Mines, and Invasion I against Gravemaw.

That version is now being moved to Godot 4.3. The Godot build already plays a whole campaign in an automated test without a single script error: the Prologue, ten countdown days with events and the scout report, an Old Mines expedition, saving and continuing, Invasion I and the end screen. Nobody has played it on screen yet. That comes next.

The move also brought two changes:

  • The map faces north. The Hollow now march down from the north edge, and Greywatch has its back to the sea cliff. The main road leads to the North gate, and a flank track runs down the east side to the East gate.
  • Tall walls. Archer Posts, Frost Bells, Ballistas and the Catapult can be built on top of the walls for extra range, and units climb up by wall stairs to fight from the walkway. If a wall falls, whatever stands on it falls too.

Next up: playing the Godot build on screen, fixing what turns up, and tuning the HUD. Then it goes to friends for feedback.

Website

The website is live

Dark Path now has a home on the web. There is no public build to play yet, so the site lets you try out the game's ideas instead.

  • The countdown. Hover over any of the 30 days to see what it holds.
  • Greywatch. Click the four rings of the city map to see what each one guards, and what you lose if it falls.
  • The torch meter. Drag the torch down and watch the dungeon get darker and more dangerous.
  • The Bestiary. Hunt the Hollow to learn their names, types and counters.

Turn on the sound in the top bar to hear strings in the city, a choir in the dungeons and war drums near the Hollow.

Oct 19, 2026
Design

The design is frozen at v1.0

The game design document for Dark Path reached version 1.0. It describes the whole game, from the surprise siege on Day 1 to the last invasion, and it is now frozen: new ideas go to a backlog until the full game ships.

Getting there took twenty revisions. Some of the bigger decisions along the way:

  • A new name. The game became Dark Path, and the last city was renamed from Ashwall to Greywatch.
  • Death is permanent on every difficulty. It started as a rule for the hardest mode only.
  • Autosave only. One save per campaign and no reloads, so every death counts.
  • A surprise siege on Day 1. The Prologue throws you into a fight before you have built anything, and losing it does not end the game.
  • Survival is the only reward. Nothing carries over between campaigns. Your score is the number of citizens still alive.
  • Shelters, evacuation and siege beasts. The last additions before the freeze.

Every feature is now tagged Core, meaning it ships in the full game, or Later, meaning it comes after launch or is cut if time runs out. The count stands at 19 Core and 15 Later.

Next up: building it.
